Stage 6 of 7

Challenge a method

Under what condition, that you identified yourself, does a sampling method become less reliable, and does the evidence agree?

Learning objective
Form a testable hypothesis about how a selection rule interacts with a population's values or order, test it fairly with repeated samples, and interpret the result, whether or not it supports you.
Reader
The health professional, who should be able to change your conditions themselves and see when the method struggles.
Data
The baseline population, data/{{sc file}}, and a labelled modified copy {{help modified-population}} that your users control. Same total n across methods and conditions.

Before you consult your agent: write your hypothesis Required first

Do this alone, before any conversation with your agent about this stage. Write it in DECISIONS.md, section 5, with today's date. Think about these questions; this guide does not answer them.

  • Which method will you challenge: simple random, systematic or stratified?
  • What change to your population, or to its ordering, could make that method less reliable?
  • Why would that method's selection rule interact with the change? What does the rule “see”, and what does it miss?
  • What evidence do you expect across repeated samples: more bias, more spread, both, or neither? For which methods?
  • What result would show that your hypothesis is wrong?

Your hypothesis does not have to succeed. An unsupported prediction, honestly tested and explained, is a valid outcome.

Required minimum Required

{{checklist s6-req - Your hypothesis and reasoning, written before consulting your agent, and a recorded prediction of the evidence. - A baseline: the supplied population, labelled, recoverable at any time. {{help baseline}} - User-controlled changes to the conditions you identified, applied to a clearly labelled modified copy. {{help modified-population}} - The true mean recalculated whenever population values or membership change. {{help true-mean}} - Repeated-sampling evidence (at least 1,000 repetitions per method and condition) with bias, SD and RMSE, at the same total n across methods and conditions. {{help rmse}} - A comparison with an alternative method, or a correction you justify. - Your own explanation of whether the results support your hypothesis. }}

Try it yourself: test your own controls

  • Return to the baseline. Do your stage 4 numbers come back (same seed, same n)?
  • Change your condition. Does the label switch to modified, and does the true mean update when, and only when, it should?
  • Check that n is identical across methods and conditions.
  • Re-run with two other seeds. Does your conclusion hold, or did it depend on one run?

Claims to avoid

  • “Random sampling is biased.” Simple random sampling is unbiased for the mean; one bad draw is variability.
  • “Systematic sampling always fails.” Any result depends on the row order and the condition you tested.
  • Calling wrong stratum weights a failure of stratification. That is a calculation error.
  • Deliberately corrupting a sampling rule, or comparing conditions at different n, to produce a failure.
  • Judging a method from one unlucky draw instead of repeated estimates, bias and RMSE.

Common questions

Why can't my agent help me come up with the hypothesis?

Devising the hypothesis is the critical reasoning this stage assesses. Your agent can help you build, check and interpret the test once your idea is written.

Does reordering rows change the true mean?

No: the same values in a different order have the same mean. Changing values, or adding or removing rows, does change it, so it must be recalculated.

My hypothesis wasn't supported. What now?

That is a valid result. Check the test was fair (same n, enough repetitions, correct rules and weights, true mean recalculated). Then explain what the evidence shows and what else might explain it. If you revise your hypothesis, add the revision below the original rather than replacing it.

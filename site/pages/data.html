Calculate the true mean

Use any method you can check: open the CSV in a spreadsheet and average the whole target column, or run a short script. To confirm the file loads and see its columns, run the starter's loader in a terminal in your project folder:

node load_data.mjs data/your CSV file
Check your answer (open after you have calculated it)
  • Clinic waiting times: mean wait_time over {{num clinic.n_rows}} appointments = {{mean clinic}}.
  • Healthcare costs: mean charges over {{num costs.n_rows}} people = {{mean costs}}.
  • Wearable heart rate: mean heart_rate_bpm over {{num heart.n_rows}} recorded minutes = {{mean heart}}.

If yours differs, check you included every row, used the target column, and did not average a column of IDs.

Try it yourself

Without looking at the card, explain to yourself in two sentences what one row is and what the true mean of your target describes. Then name one group of people, places or times your population does not describe.

Claims to avoid

  • “This is the average for all patients” (or all clinics, all days, all wearers). It is the average of this file.
  • Calling the simulated clinic data or the cost teaching data real patient records.
  • Treating the heart-rate rows as evenly spaced minutes.

Common questions

Why is a whole dataset called a “population” here?

Because you can measure all of it, you know the true mean exactly, so you can see how close each sampling method gets. In real work you rarely have this luxury.

Is the clinic file the same as the one in the course notebook?

No. The notebook's clinic_visits.csv was not available, so this one was newly simulated with the same layout (10,000 appointments: 100 weeks, Monday–Friday, 20 slots a day, columns visit_id, slot, visit_type, wait_time, plus week and weekday). Its values, and so its true mean, differ from any numbers quoted in the notebook. Use the numbers from this file.

Why do some heart-rate minutes have no row?

The device recorded nothing in those minutes, for example while it was off the wrist or charging. Those minutes are not in your population. Your true mean is the mean of the recorded minutes.

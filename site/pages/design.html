Design your app

Can someone see the whole population, which rows were sampled, and how the sample's estimate compares with the truth?

Learning objective
Design a display that makes a sample {{help sample}} visible inside its population, and compare a sample mean {{help sample-mean}} with the true mean {{help true-mean}}.
Reader
A health professional who understands {{sc title}} but is new to sampling.
Data
data/{{sc file}}: all {{sc n}} {{sc rows}}; target {{sc target}} in {{sc units}}.

Finished result

bash run_app.sh opens your first app: all {{sc n}} {{sc rows}} of the population, a temporary random sample of 100 highlighted within it, and the sample mean beside the labelled true mean, in your chosen design.

Decide

Finish stage 1 and setup first. Any visual form is allowed, as long as it shows the full population {{help population}}, which rows were sampled, and the sample estimate {{help estimate}} beside the labelled true mean. You decide:

  • which of your agent's three visualization ideas to build, and why it suits your reader and your data;
  • one visual feature meant to help reveal a sampling problem, and what it should help a user learn.

Optional Styling, animations and extra features are never required. Essential and optional

More help

Why a temporary random sample?

It lets you test the display before the three methods exist. Stage 3 replaces it with simple random, systematic and stratified sampling.

My agent suggested a design I like better than my own idea. Can I use it?

Yes. Choosing well is the skill. Record why it suits your reader and your data, and what it is meant to help them learn.

My agent started editing files in Plan mode.

Stop it and check your mode indicator. Ask it to list and undo the changes it just made, or restore a file from your last commit with git restore <file> (this discards that file's uncommitted changes, so never use it on DECISIONS.md unless you mean to). Then restart the discussion in Plan mode.

Claims to avoid
  • “The sample mean is close to the true mean, so the method works.” One sample cannot show that.
  • An unlabelled line or number that could be either the sample mean or the true mean.

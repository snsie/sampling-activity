Individual activity

Judge the Sample

Build an interactive app with a coding agent to explore three sampling methods using one health dataset. You choose the design, predict the results, and explain what the evidence shows. Your agent writes and debugs the code.

Which sampling method would you trust for this population, at what sample size, and with what limits?

At a glance

Who
You, individually. No partner or peer testing is needed.
Data
Choose one supplied population: clinic waiting times (the default), healthcare costs, or wearable heart rate. The whole CSV is your finite population {{help population}}. Data & files
Build
One app, developed across stages 2–5, in a stack you agree with your agent. Its design is yours.
Investigate
Three sampling methods compared at n = 100, how sample size changes reliability, and a challenge you devise yourself.
Audience
A health professional who understands your scenario but is new to sampling.
Submit
Your app and source code, your scenario and seed(s), a results table, and three short written findings: the methods compared, the effect of sample size, and your challenge. Submit evidence

How you work with your agent

  • Your agent writes and debugs the code. It reads {{dl AGENT_BRIEF.md}} first: the task, the sampling rules and its boundaries. {{help agent-brief}}
  • You choose and justify designs. Before each design, switch the agent to Plan mode {{help plan-mode}} to brainstorm options, pick one and say why, then switch to implementation mode {{help implementation-mode}} to build it.
  • You devise the challenge. You write your stage 5 hypothesis, and what would contradict it, yourself, before you consult your agent.
  • You check and interpret. You run the app, read the evidence and write your own explanations in {{dl DECISIONS.md}}. {{help decisions-md}}

Start your agent with the starting prompt. Each stage page has the prompts for that stage, adapted to your scenario. Stages 2–5 follow the same pattern: Your task, Plan and build, Check your app, Record your findings.

Start here

  1. Choose and understand a population. Pick one scenario, download it, and work out what one row means, the target and its true mean {{help true-mean}}. Done when: section 1 of DECISIONS.md is filled in and your true mean matches the check. Stage 1 brief
  2. Design your app. In Plan mode, brainstorm three visualizations; choose one, then build it with a temporary sample. Done when: bash run_app.sh shows the full population, the sampled rows, and the sample mean beside the true mean. Stage 2 brief
  3. Explore sampling methods. In Plan mode, brainstorm interactions; then add simple random, systematic and stratified sampling. Done when: a user can see each method's selection rule, change a setting and watch the sample and estimate update. Stage 3 brief
  4. Compare methods and sample size. Two experiments in one stage: compare the three methods at n = 100, then use simple random sampling at n = 10, 100 and 640. At least 1,000 random repetitions per condition, or exact enumeration for systematic sampling when available. Done when: your app shows each result's spread of estimates against the labelled true mean, with bias, SD and RMSE {{help rmse}}, each labelled Monte Carlo or exact. Stage 4 brief
  5. Challenge a method. Write your own hypothesis first; then build controls that test it. Done when: users can compare baseline and changed conditions with repeated sampling, plus an alternative method or correction. Stage 5 brief
  6. Submit evidence. Collect your results table and findings. Done when: every item on the final self-check is ticked. Stage 6 brief

Essential and optional

Essential: the required items on each stage page: a correct, reproducible app with readable labels, usable controls and visualizations someone else can interpret, and your own notes. Optional: experiment histories or saved runs, decorative styling, animations, elaborate layouts and extra app features. You never need them to finish; your app only has to show its current results with their settings, and you can record the key numbers in {{dl DECISIONS.md}}.

Your progress

Ticks on these pages and your scenario choice are saved in this browser only. They are reminders, not submission evidence, and every page stays open in any order. Your real record is your own notes file, {{dl DECISIONS.md}}, in your project.

Statistical integrity and AI use

  • The file is the population. Your true mean describes the rows in your CSV, not all patients, all clinics or all days.
  • Keep the rules correct. Creativity applies to how your app explains and explores the methods, never to their definitions. Your agent follows the shared sampling rules in AGENT_BRIEF.md.
  • Judge methods on many samples. Use repeated estimates, bias, SD and RMSE, not one lucky or unlucky draw. Label results Monte Carlo (random repetitions, with a seed) or exact (exact enumeration).
  • Test fairly. A hypothesis that isn't supported is a valid result to explain; never change a sampling rule to force an outcome.
  • Your words, your decisions. Your agent does not write your notes, hypothesis or explanations. Record which AI tools you used and what you checked yourself. Follow your course's AI policy for anything not covered here.

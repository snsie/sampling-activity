Individual activity

Judge the Sample

Choose one health population, then build your own app, with a coding agent writing the code, that shows how simple random, systematic and stratified sampling estimate its mean. You make the design decisions, the predictions and the judgements.

Which sampling method would you trust for this population, at what sample size, and with what limits?

At a glance

Who
You, individually. No partner or peer testing is needed.
Data
Choose one supplied population: clinic waiting times (the default), healthcare costs, or wearable heart rate. The whole CSV is your finite population {{help population}}. Data & files
Build
One app, developed across stages 2–6, in a stack you agree with your agent. Its design is yours.
Investigate
Three sampling methods, repeated samples, sample size, and a challenge you devise yourself.
Audience
A health professional who understands your scenario but is new to sampling.
Submit
Your app and source code, your scenario and seed, three comparisons (baseline, sample size, challenge), and your own explanations. Submit evidence

How you work with your agent

  • Your agent writes and debugs the code. It reads {{dl AGENT_BRIEF.md}} first: the task, the sampling rules and its boundaries. {{help agent-brief}}
  • You choose and justify designs. Before each design, you switch the agent to Plan mode {{help plan-mode}} to brainstorm options, pick one and say why, then switch to implementation mode {{help implementation-mode}} to build it.
  • You predict before you test. Predictions go in your notes before any experiment runs.
  • You devise the challenge. Your stage 6 hypothesis is written by you, before you consult your agent.
  • You check and interpret. You run the app yourself, read the evidence and write your own explanations in {{dl DECISIONS.md}}. {{help decisions-md}}

Start your agent with the starting prompt. Each stage page has the prompts for that stage, adapted to your scenario.

Start here

  1. Choose and understand a population. Pick one scenario, download it, and work out what one row means, the target and its true mean {{help true-mean}}. Done when: section 1 of DECISIONS.md is filled in and your true mean matches the check. Stage 1 brief
  2. Design your app. In Plan mode, brainstorm three visualizations; choose one, then build it with a temporary sample. Done when: bash run_app.sh shows the full population, the sampled rows, and the sample mean beside the true mean. Stage 2 brief
  3. Explore sampling methods. In Plan mode, brainstorm interactions; then add simple random, systematic and stratified sampling. Done when: a user can see each method's selection rule, change a setting and watch the sample and estimate update. Stage 3 brief
  4. Compare repeated samples. Predict which method will do best, then add at least 1,000 repetitions per method. Done when: your app shows each method's spread of estimates against the labelled true mean, with bias and RMSE {{help rmse}}. Stage 4 brief
  5. Change sample size. Predict, then let users compare repeated samples at several sizes. Done when: users can compare at least 10, 40, 160 and 640 with 1,000 repetitions each. Stage 5 brief
  6. Challenge a method. Write your own hypothesis first; then build controls that test it. Done when: users can compare baseline and changed conditions, with repeated-sampling evidence and an alternative method or correction. Stage 6 brief
  7. Submit evidence. Collect your comparisons and explanations. Done when: every item on the final self-check is ticked. Stage 7 brief

Your progress

Ticks on these pages and your scenario choice are saved in this browser only. They are reminders, not submission evidence, and every page stays open in any order. Your real record is your own notes file, {{dl DECISIONS.md}}, in your project.

Statistical integrity and AI use

  • The file is the population. Your true mean describes the rows in your CSV, not all patients, all clinics or all days.
  • Keep the rules correct. Creativity applies to how your app explains and explores the methods, never to their definitions. Every display uses the same shared calculation functions.
  • Judge methods on many samples. Use repeated estimates, bias and RMSE, not one lucky or unlucky draw.
  • Never manufacture a failure. A hypothesis that isn't supported is a valid result to explain.
  • Your words, your decisions. Your agent does not write your notes, predictions, hypothesis or explanations. Record which AI tools you used and what you checked yourself. Follow your course's AI policy for anything not covered here.

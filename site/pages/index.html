Individual activity

Judge the Sample

Build an interactive app with a coding agent to explore three sampling methods using one health dataset. You choose the design, predict the results, and explain what the evidence shows. Your agent writes and debugs the code.

Which sampling method would you trust for this population, at what sample size, and with what limits?

At a glance

Who
You, individually. No partner or peer testing is needed.
Data
Choose one supplied population: clinic waiting times (the default), healthcare costs, or wearable heart rate. The whole CSV is your finite population {{help population}}. Data & files
Build
One app, developed across stages 2–5, as a JavaScript web app (we suggest React with Vite). Its design is yours.
Investigate
Three sampling methods compared at n = 100, how sample size changes reliability, and a challenge you devise yourself.
Audience
A health professional who understands your scenario but is new to sampling.
Submit
Your app and source code, your scenario and seed(s), a results table, and three short written findings: the method comparison, the sample-size finding and the challenge interpretation. Submit evidence

How you work with your agent

  • Your agent writes and debugs the code. It reads {{dl AGENT_BRIEF.md}} first: the task, the sampling rules and its boundaries. {{help agent-brief}}
  • You choose and justify designs. Before each design, switch the agent to Plan mode {{help plan-mode}} to brainstorm options, pick one and say why, then switch to implementation mode {{help implementation-mode}} to build it.
  • You devise the challenge. You write your stage 5 hypothesis, and what would contradict it, yourself, before you consult your agent.
  • Your agent verifies the software; you interpret the evidence. Your agent may run automated correctness checks and report whether they pass. You run the investigation, read the evidence and write your own explanations in {{dl DECISIONS.md}}. {{help decisions-md}}

Start your agent with the starting prompt. Each stage page has the prompts for that stage, adapted to your scenario. Stages 2–5 start with a Finished result and follow the same steps: Decide what you will choose, Plan with your agent in Plan mode, Build in implementation mode, Check the running app, and Record in the named section of DECISIONS.md.

Start here

  1. Choose and understand a population. Pick one scenario, download it, and work out what one row means, the target and its true mean {{help true-mean}}. Done when: section 1 of DECISIONS.md is filled in and your true mean matches the check. Stage 1 brief
  2. Design your app. In Plan mode, brainstorm three visualizations; choose one, record it, then build it with a temporary sample. Done when: bash run_app.sh shows the full population, the sampled rows, and the sample mean beside the true mean. Stage 2 brief
  3. Explore sampling methods. In Plan mode, compare interaction approaches; then add simple random, systematic and stratified sampling. Done when: a user can see each method's selection rule, change a setting and watch the sample and estimate update. Stage 3 brief
  4. Compare methods and sample size. Two experiments in one stage: compare the three methods at n = 100, then use simple random sampling at n = 1, 10 and 100. At least 100 random repetitions per condition (default 100, with a control to increase to 1,000); exact enumeration for systematic sampling with a whole-number interval. Done when: your app shows each result's spread of estimates against the labelled true mean, with bias, SD of estimates and RMSE {{help rmse}}, each labelled Monte Carlo or exact; table rows and findings (a) and (b) are recorded. Stage 4 brief
  5. Challenge a method. Write your own dated hypothesis first; then build controls that test it. Done when: at the same n, your chosen method and one alternative or correction each run on the baseline and modified populations, each compared with its own true mean, and finding (c) is recorded. Stage 5 brief
  6. Submit evidence. Check your results table, three findings and supporting screenshots. Done when: every item on the final self-check is ticked. Stage 6 brief

Your progress

Ticks on these pages and your scenario choice are saved in this browser only. They are reminders, not submission evidence, and every page stays open in any order. Your real record is your own notes file, {{dl DECISIONS.md}}, in your project.

Statistical integrity and AI use

  • The file is the population. Your true mean describes the rows in your CSV, not all patients, all clinics or all days.
  • Keep the rules correct. Creativity applies to how your app explains and explores the methods, never to their definitions. Your agent follows the shared sampling rules in AGENT_BRIEF.md.
  • Judge methods on many samples. Use repeated estimates, bias, SD and RMSE, not one lucky or unlucky draw. Label results Monte Carlo (random repetitions, with a seed) or exact (exact enumeration).
  • Test fairly. A hypothesis that isn't supported is a valid result to explain; never change a sampling rule to force an outcome.
  • Your words, your decisions. Your agent may explain terms, ask clarifying questions and verify the code, but it does not write your notes, hypothesis, findings or explanations.

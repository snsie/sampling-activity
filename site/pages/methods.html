More help

Systematic sampling when N/n is not a whole number

There is no whole-number interval, so use the fractional-interval design: k = N/n, a random start u between 0 and k, and rows ceil(u + j·k) for j = 0 to n − 1. It gives exactly n distinct rows (k is at least 1) and the same chance n/N for every row. Do not restrict n to sizes where N/n is whole: the sizes you need later would not work. How do I round a proportional allocation?

Give each stratum about n × Nh/N rows, round down, then hand the leftover rows to the strata with the largest remainders. Check that each stratum gets at least one row, none gets more rows than it has, and the total is exactly n. If n is smaller than the number of strata, one row per stratum is impossible: tell your agent and decide together what to do.

Why weight the stratum means?

Each stratum's sample mean estimates that stratum's mean. Weighting by Nh/N, each stratum's share of the population, combines them into an estimate of the whole population's mean. Without the weights, strata that got slightly more rows from rounding would count too much.

Claims to avoid
  • “Systematic sampling isn't random.” With a random start, every row has the same chance of selection. {{help inclusion-probability}}
  • Averaging all stratified rows together without weights, and calling that the stratified estimate.
  • Different sample sizes for different methods in the same comparison.
  • Changing a method's definition to make the app look more interesting.

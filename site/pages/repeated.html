Claims to avoid

  • Calling a method biased because one sample missed the true mean.
  • Treating a small Monte Carlo bias that changes sign with the seed as evidence of real bias.
  • “Smaller spread means no bias.” Spread and bias are separate questions.
  • Calling a method the best without saying for which population, which n, and by which measure.

Common questions

What does RMSE add to bias and SD?

RMSE is the typical size of the error of one estimate, counting both a consistent offset (bias) and random scatter (SD): RMSE² = bias² + SD². It is in the same units as your target, so you can say “a typical estimate is off by about … {{sc units}}”.

Why can systematic sampling be enumerated exactly?

With a whole-number interval k, the random start is the only random step, and there are only k starts. Listing all k samples gives the exact distribution. Drawing 1,000 random starts just repeats those k samples many times. For the clinic file at n = 100, k = 10,000 / 100 = 100.

My prediction was wrong. Is that a problem?

No. Keep it as written and add, under observations, what happened and what you think explains the difference.
